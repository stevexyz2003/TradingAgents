### Bull Researcher

Bull Analyst: **Bull Case for TSLA: Revenue Is Re-Accelerating, the Stock Has Higher Lows, and the Bear's Best Points Are Already Priced Into a Pullback**

I'll start with the bear case, since it has real evidence behind it. Margins are compressing, operating income fell 57% year over year in Q2 2026, and free cash flow turned negative as capex ramped. The stock is also below its 200-day SMA, and the 10 EMA has rolled over. I don't dismiss any of that. My argument is that these facts describe a company in a heavy investment phase, and the price already reflects much of the damage.

**1. The top line is recovering, and the trend is stronger than the bear admits.**
Q2 2026 revenue was $28.2B, up 25.5% YoY. Q1 was up 15.8%, and H1 2026 revenue of $50.6B is up 21%. The bear will say the comparison base was weak. Q2 2025 was down 11.8%, so some of this growth is easy. I accept that. But growth that goes from -11.8% to +15.8% to +25.5% is a trajectory, not a single data point. Demand is not deteriorating.

**2. Cash generation at the operating level has improved sharply.**
Operating cash flow for H1 2026 was $8.6B, up 84% from $4.7B a year earlier. The bear points to negative Q2 free cash flow of about -$1.1B. But that negative number comes from capex of $5.8B in one quarter. Operating cash generation is what supports the build-out, and it is moving the right way. A company that is cutting cash generation to fund growth looks very different from one whose core business is failing.

**3. The balance sheet is not in distress.**
Equity rose 9% to $86.9B. Liabilities-to-equity is 0.70, and the current ratio is 1.94. The bear is right that cash fell 17% and current liabilities are growing faster than current assets, and those are trends to monitor. But a 1.94 current ratio and moderate leverage are not the profile of a company that needs to raise capital in a panic.

**4. The margin problem is real, but the Q1 result shows what is possible.**
The bear's strongest argument is the 1.4% operating margin in Q2. I won't wave that away. But gross margin was 21.1% in Q1 2026, so the company has recently run at a much higher level of profitability. The question is whether Q2 reflects temporary ramp costs and investment spending or a permanent loss of pricing power. The data cannot answer that.
